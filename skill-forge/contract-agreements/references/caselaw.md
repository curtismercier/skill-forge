> **Last verified:** 2026-05-27. Caselaw is subject to being overturned or distinguished —
> verify on [CanLII](https://www.canlii.org) before relying on any decision.

## Quantum Meruit (Unjust Enrichment)

Used when compensation is claimed for work performed without a formal contract.
Relevant for settlement agreements where work was performed before a written agreement.

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Deglman v. Guaranty Trust Co.** | [1954] SCR 725 ([CanLII](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/1954/1954canlii2/1954canlii2.html)) | Quantum meruit available where services rendered without a contract — compensation measured by the value of the benefit conferred. |
| **Pettkus v. Becker** | [1980] 2 SCR 834 ([CanLII](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/1980/1980canlii22/1980canlii22.html)) | Three-part test for unjust enrichment: (1) enrichment, (2) corresponding deprivation, (3) absence of juristic reason for the enrichment. |
| **Garland v. Consumers' Gas Co.** | [2004] 3 SCR 629 ([CanLII](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2004/2004scc25/2004scc25.html)) | "Absence of juristic reason" test refined — claimant must show no established category of juristic reason applies. |

**Application:** When drafting an invoice or settlement for work done without a signed contract,
reference quantum meruit as the legal basis for compensation. The work performed, accepted
by the client, and unpaid is the common starting point.

## Contract Formation & Good Faith

| Case | Citation | Holding |
|---|---|---|
| **Bhasin v. Hrynew** | [2014] 3 SCR 494 ([CanLII](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2014/2014scc71/2014scc71.html)) | SCC recognized a duty of honest contractual performance — parties must not lie or mislead in performing contractual obligations. Implied organizing principle of good faith. |
| **Martel Building Ltd. v. Canada** | [2000] 2 SCR 860 ([CanLII](https://www.canlii.org/en/ca/scc/doc/2000/2000scc60/2000scc60.html)) | Duty of care in contract negotiations. Relevant for pre-contractual representations. |

**Application:** Good faith obligations can be referenced in recitals to establish
the expected standard of conduct. Relevant when drafting settlement clauses around
honest disclosure of assets or work-completion status.

## Quick Reference

| Topic | Key Case | Short Holding |
|---|---|---|
| Unpaid work | Deglman [1954] | Quantum meruit available without contract |
| Unjust enrichment | Pettkus [1980] | 3-part test: enrichment + deprivation + no reason |
| Unjust enrichment test refined | Garland [2004] | Claimant must rebut established juristic reasons |
| Good faith in contracts | Bhasin [2014] | Duty of honest contractual performance |
| Limitation period | Limitations Act, 2002 | 2 years from discovery |
| Small Claims limit | Courts of Justice Act s. 23 | $35,000 (2026) |
