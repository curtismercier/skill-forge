# Ontario Law Reference for Contracts & Agreements

> **Last verified:** 2026-05-27. Ontario statutes can be amended — verify against
> [Ontario e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws) before relying on a specific section.

---

## Statutes

### Courts of Justice Act, R.S.O. 1990, c. C.43

| Section | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **s. 128** | Pre-judgment interest rate. If payment terms don't specify interest, the default rate under this section applies. Used in demand letters and settlement terms to establish interest on overdue amounts. |
| **s. 129** | Post-judgment interest. Relevant when drafting settlement terms that reference court-ordered amounts. |
| **s. 23** | Small Claims Court jurisdiction. Limit is $35,000 (as of 2026). Relevant for determining forum for disputes. |

### Limitations Act, 2002, S.O. 2002, c. 24, Sched. B

| Section | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **s. 4** | Basic limitation period: 2 years from discovery of claim. Relevant for mutual release language — releases typically cover claims up to the date of the agreement. |
| **s. 5** | Discovery of claim. Defines when a claim is "discovered" for limitation purposes. |
| **s. 15** | No limitation period for certain claims (e.g., assault, sexual assault). Important carve-out for mutual release language. |

### Residential Tenancies Act, 2006, S.O. 2006, c. 17

- Not typically relevant to developer-client contracts — included for completeness.

### Copyright Act, R.S.C. 1985, c. C-42 (Federal)

| Section | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **s. 13(1)** | Ownership of copyright vests in the author (developer) unless assigned in writing. IP assignment must be in writing and signed. |
| **s. 13(3)** | In the case of work made in the course of employment, the employer owns the copyright. Distinguish employee vs. independent contractor for IP ownership. |
| **s. 13(4)** | Assignment of copyright must be in writing and signed by the owner. Oral agreements are insufficient. |

### Ontario Digital Service Act (if applicable)

- Not typically relevant to private contracts — included for awareness.

## Key Principles

### Independent Legal Advice (ILA)

Settlement agreements and releases should include an acknowledgment that each party
has had the opportunity to obtain independent legal advice. This is particularly
important when:
- One party is an individual (not a corporation) waiving rights
- The settlement involves a payment significantly less than the claimed amount
- A mutual release of claims is involved

### Consideration

Every contract under Ontario law requires consideration (something of value exchanged).
For settlement agreements, the payment itself is consideration. For IP assignments,
the payment is consideration. For mutual releases, the mutual promises to release
claims serve as consideration for each other.

### Writing Requirements

| Document type | Writing required? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| IP assignment | ✅ Yes (Copyright Act s. 13(4)) | Must be in writing and signed |
| Settlement agreement | ✅ Recommended | Enforceability is stronger with signed writing |
| Mutual release under seal | ✅ Recommended | A seal can extend the limitation period to 20 years |
| Service contract | ✅ Recommended if >1 year | Statute of Frauds requires writing for agreements not performable within one year |

### Small Claims Court (Ontario)

- Limit: $35,000 (2026)
- Rule 19 of O. Reg. 258/98 governs Offers to Settle
- Rule 19 cost consequences apply if a party refuses an offer and the court awards an equal or greater amount
- Parties can self-represent (lawyer not required)
- No discovery process (simplified procedure)

## Common Onto-About Boilerplate

- **Governing law:** "This Agreement is governed by the laws of Ontario and the federal laws of Canada applicable therein."
- **Dispute resolution:** "Any dispute arising out of this Agreement shall first be submitted to mediation before a mutually agreed mediator, and if not resolved within 30 days, may be referred to the Ontario Small Claims Court."
- **Interest on overdue amounts:** "Any amounts not paid when due shall bear interest at the rate of [X]% per annum, or the rate prescribed under s. 128 of the Courts of Justice Act, whichever is higher."
